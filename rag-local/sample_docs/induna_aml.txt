This section identifies and assesses the mitigating measures in place, both at supervisory and control levels, to reduce ML/TF inherent risk. These are grouped into four main areas: (1) ML/TF risk assessment; (2) AML/CFT Supervision; (3) ongoing monitoring and (4) procedures and trainings.

Promoting a strict compliance culture throughout the organisation, especially in the first line of defence; 
Performing at least yearly AML/CFT trainings for all staff members, including typologies relevant to the TCSP industry and an update of the Specialised PFS internal procedure;

Performing transaction monitoring by using automated systems (except when the professional can prove that the volume and nature of the customers and the transactions to be supervised do not require such automation) including rules of volume, pattern and frequency; 
Ensuring close oversight over branches, subsidiaries and all delegates and service providers performing AML/CFT controls on behalf of the Specialised PFS; 
Providing control functions (especially Compliance) with the necessary authority, independence, means and management support; 
Ensuring clear allocation of responsibilities between 1st and 2nd lines of defence; 
Ensuring an appropriate “tone from the top” such that there is direct participation of the management body in the AML/CFT strategy and framework definition, including regular reporting.

# 1 Take appropriate steps to identify and assess firm-wide ML/TF risks
# 2 Integrate the information provided in this Sub-Sector Risk Assessment, NRA and SNRA in the internal risk assessments
# 3 Implement a clear AML/CFT risk appetite and strategy
# 4 Employ robust CDD process to reliably identify beneficial ownership and critically appraises origin of funds/source of wealth
# 5 Adopt enhanced measures for clients identified as high risk, including entities whose legal structure has been altered frequently and/or without adequate explanation
# 6 Review client relationships on a periodic basis to determine whether ML/TF risk has changed
# 7 Ensure appropriate additional controls are in place where 3rd parties are used for CDD
# 8 Ensure that name screening against Targeted Financial Sanctions screening is performed immediately as required notably by EU regulations

# 12 Continue to collaborate closely with the Luxembourg competent authorities

2.5 Legislation and regulation update
Law of 30th March 2020 - Transposition of the 5th Directive
The new law amending AML/CFT 2004 Law broadens the scope of AML regulation:
to every person [in each financial institution being] subject to the control of the CSSF for anti-money laundering purposes, to agents, to real estate agents, tax advisor, VASP, art works agent;
expands the signification of ‘control by other means’ of a beneficial owner over a client which is a corporate entity: 
the power to exercise a dominant influence by way of an article of a contract or the articles of association;
the power to appoint or remove a majority of the management body.

 to real estate agents, tax advisor, VASP, art works agent;
expands the signification of ‘control by other means’ of a beneficial owner over a client which is a corporate entity: 
the power to exercise a dominant influence by way of an article of a contract or the articles of association;
the power to appoint or remove a majority of the management body.

Some anti-money laundering obligations were also reinforced:
to understand the purpose and the nature of the business relationships;
transactions including the involvement of high-risk countries, as well as complex or unusual transactions require enhanced due diligence;
all FP to fulfill registration obligations (UBO register & Fiduciary and trust register)

Definition of Money Laundering

Money laundering is the injection into the financial system of funds that are derived from illegal activities in order to give them the appearance of legitimacy

Two important concepts:
- Illegal activities --> predicate offences
- Use of the financial system for the placement, layering and integration of black money, which as a result becomes white

- Use of the financial system for the placement, layering and integration of black money, which as a result becomes white

Definition of terrorist financing:

Terrorist financing is the reverse process of money laundering, as it usually consists in the use of legitimate funds in order to promote terrorist -i.e. criminal- activities
In other words, white money which is turned black

Predicate offences:

Since the entry into force of the Law of July 17th, 2008 transposing Directive 2005/60/EC, criminal offences which result in a penalty of at least six months of imprisonment are considered as predicate offences.

Since the entry into force of the Law of July 18th, 2014 on cybercrime, another three criminal offences have been added to the list of predicate offences, namely interception of computer data, misuse of devices and misuse of electronic signature

Since the entry into force of the tax reform Law of December 23rd, 2016, two tax-related criminal offences have been added to the list of predicate offences, namely serious tax fraud and tax swindle

Sanctions / Breaches of professional obligations:

Administrative measures and sanctions:
- warning; reprimand; public statement identifying the natural or legal person and the nature of the breach; withdrawal or suspension of the license; a temporary ban to exercise a professional activity within the financial services industry or to exercise managerial functions
